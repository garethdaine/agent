# Agentic Economy Research Report (Global)
Date: 3 March 2026  
Prepared for: investor deck development (AI agents, agentic workflows, agent startups)

## 1) Executive Summary

- The market has moved from “AI pilots” to scaled deployment and commercialization.
- Enterprise demand is measurable:
  - McKinsey reports **78%** of organizations now use AI in at least one business function (up from 72% in early 2024).
  - Gartner forecasts **33% of enterprise software** will include agentic AI by 2028 (from <1% in 2024), and **15% of daily work decisions** will be autonomous by 2028.
  - IDC forecasts global AI spend reaching **~$632B by 2028**.
- Revenue capture is already happening at multiple layers:
  - Platform incumbents: Microsoft, Salesforce.
  - Services/SIs: Accenture.
  - Startups: Sierra, Cognition, n8n, Glean funding/revaluation momentum.
  - Bottom-up indie/operator economy: TrustMRR + X.com signals show early but real monetization in agent wrappers, hosting, and automation services.
- Key investor framing: the near-term winner is not just “best model,” but “best deployment system” (integration, governance, ROI operations, reliability).

## 2) Market Size and Adoption Signals

## 2.1 Enterprise adoption

- **McKinsey (State of AI, 2025):** 78% of organizations report AI use in at least one function.
- **Gartner (June 25, 2025):**
  - 33% of enterprise software expected to include agentic AI by 2028.
  - 15% of day-to-day work decisions expected autonomous by 2028.
  - More than 40% of agentic AI projects expected to be canceled by end-2027 due to risk/cost/value issues.

Interpretation: demand exists, but execution quality is now the bottleneck.

## 2.2 Spend expansion

- **IDC (Aug 2024 forecast):** global AI spending projected to reach **~$632B in 2028** (with infrastructure + services leading).
- **BCG (Jan 2025):**
  - GenAI could unlock **$2T+ annual revenue uplift by 2027**.
  - Approx. **$520B** of that opportunity could come from AI agents.

Interpretation: strong macro tailwind, with “agentic” specifically called out as a material revenue contributor.

## 3) Where Money Is Being Made Now

## 3.1 Enterprise and platform layer (high-confidence)

- **Microsoft (Jan 2025 earnings call):** AI business annual revenue run rate surpassed **$13B**.
- **Salesforce (Dec 2025 earnings release):**
  - Data Cloud + AI ARR surpassed **$900M**.
  - More than **5,000 Agentforce deals** since launch.
  - More than **3,000 paid Agentforce customers**.
- **Accenture (Sep 2026 FY2026 results):**
  - **$5.9B** in GenAI new bookings for FY2026.
  - **$2.7B** in GenAI revenue for FY2026.

Interpretation: budgets are real and already flowing to both software and implementation vendors.

## 3.2 Startup funding/revaluation layer (high-confidence)

- **Sierra:** raised **$175M**, valued at **$4.5B** (reported by CNBC, Jan 2025).
- **Cognition (Devin):** reported **$300M raise** at ~**$10B** valuation and acquisition of Windsurf (TechCrunch, Jul 2025).
- **n8n (agentic workflow automation infra):** raised **€55M (~$60M)**, valuation around **€250M (~$270M)** (TechCrunch, Nov 2024).
- **Glean:** raised **$150M** at **$7.2B** valuation (TechCrunch, Jun 2025).

Interpretation: investors are funding both “agent applications” and “agent infrastructure/orchestration.”

## 3.3 Bottom-up operator economy (mixed confidence, still valuable)

### High-confidence (direct platform data)

- **TrustMRR OpenClaw live snapshot (crawled Mar 2026):**
  - **138 startups**
  - **$305,322** verified revenue in last 30 days
  - Implied average about **$2.2K/startup/30 days** (distribution likely highly skewed).
- TrustMRR also positions itself as a large verified startup revenue database and publishes per-startup verified figures.

### Medium-confidence (X posts that point to TrustMRR-backed numbers)

- Bookmarked and live X posts show recurring OpenClaw/TrustMRR summaries:
  - 125 startups / $274K in 30 days (Feb 28, 2026).
  - 137 startups / $302K in 30 days (Mar 2026 reposts/threads).
- These are useful ecosystem pulse indicators but should be treated as amplification of TrustMRR, not standalone audited financial reporting.

### Low-confidence (X “how to make money” claims)

- Common claim ranges on X for AI automation agencies:
  - Setup fees roughly **$800 to $5,000+**
  - Monthly retainers roughly **$300 to $800+** (sometimes higher claims like $10K/mo retainers)
- X also contains explicit skepticism/counter-signals (for example, “grifter” narratives and “$0/10k MRR” build-in-public logs).

Interpretation: the opportunity is real, but X pricing narratives are noisy and often marketing-led.

## 3.4 Freelance/marketplace pricing (directional)

- Upwork category pages indicate broad market demand and substantial hourly spread for AI talent.
- Reported ranges vary by role/skill depth and are typically presented as broad bands (for example, AI engineering and AI development category ranges).

Interpretation: services revenue is accessible at the SMB/mid-market layer, but pricing discipline and niche positioning drive outcomes.

## 4) What This Means for an Investor Deck

## 4.1 Deck thesis (recommended)

- “The agentic market is not blocked by model capability anymore; it is blocked by deployment quality.”
- Winners will combine:
  - software leverage (platform),
  - operational expertise (implementation),
  - recurring optimization loops (managed service/retainer).

## 4.2 Suggested narrative arc

1. Demand inflection: AI adoption and spend are already mainstream.
2. Execution gap: high cancellation risk in agent projects creates need for specialist operators.
3. Revenue proof: incumbents + SIs + startup ecosystem + bottom-up creators are all monetizing now.
4. Business model: license + implementation + recurring optimization retainer.
5. Defensibility: workflow templates, reliability tooling, governance, integrations, vertical playbooks.

## 4.3 Investor-grade positioning

- Position the company as an **agent operations platform + delivery system**, not just an “agent builder.”
- Emphasize measurable outcomes:
  - time saved,
  - cost removed,
  - conversion/revenue lift,
  - SLA/reliability.
- Show how service revenue bootstraps distribution while software drives long-term margin expansion.

## 5) Risk and Diligence Notes

- Social proof risk: many X posts are promotional; use confidence tiers and triangulate against primary data.
- Survivorship bias: TrustMRR/indie showcases overrepresent public builders willing to share numbers.
- Enterprise risk: project cancellation rates are non-trivial; governance and ROI instrumentation are essential.
- Platform dependency: startup outcomes can be sensitive to upstream model pricing/policy changes.
